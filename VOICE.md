# VOICE.md — how Jeremy actually sounds

This file outranks every style rule when they disagree. The rules in
CLAUDE.md still hold for facts (no fabrication, no em dashes, no invented
numbers). For tone, this is the reference, and it is made of specimens
rather than rules because rules only prevent failures. Specimens show the
target.

Every line below is something Jeremy really typed, pulled from four
months of him directing this site. Nothing is cleaned up. That is the
point.

## The one test

Read the line out loud. If you have never heard a person say a sentence
shaped like that, it does not ship. Jeremy's own words, the day he
caught "Same desk for all of it": *"a real person wouldn't say that...in
fact i've never heard a person say that before."*

## The register, in his words

> "i want it to be warm...this site is FUN to me, i'm over the moon
> excited about all we can do with AI right now that i can't stop - it's
> fun to be on the computer again after about 10 years of sameness,
> honestly. It's like back in the Flash animation days or something"

> "i'm not a screamy in your face type - but it's the sense of
> fun/urgency they can feel...i want a little of that here."

> Coworkers, watching him talk about this stuff: "you seem like you want
> to do this full time..."

That is the brief. Warm. Quietly excited. The excitement is felt, never
performed.

## Specimens

### How he gets excited

- "what if instead of the logo sections being stacked they were in a carousel - just a simple auto side swipe? might be kinda cool!"
- "this is really coming along!"
- "love it! can the text below it have the same affect?"
- "let's go richer!"
- "it might be interesting down there as a 'surprise'"
- "just trying to make something interesting!"
- "even seeing ivy park there - i can tell the logos would be better! let me find the others and get them to you"
- "i was thinking about this a lot and instead of a different section what if we utilize the notes section we already have at the top!?"
- "it would just sort of be a fun experiment to see if i could style out some really cool 'inbox'"
- "oh the cowboy hat emoji - well played claude, well played :-)"

One exclamation point at a time. Excitement lands on ideas, not on
adjectives. "might be kinda cool" is peak enthusiasm, and it is enough.

### How he decides

- "let's do a 35 and call it good!"
- "B for sure on question 1."
- "fixed is totally fine"
- "location is great"
- "yep - let's do it for all of them now, ty!"
- "oh good call - go ahead and convert them and delete the ttf"
- "best of both worlds that way"
- "let's do work it into the career card and see how it looks"

Decisions are casual and fast. No ceremony around them.

### How he thinks out loud

- "i wonder what it would look like if we kept the homepage template for the daybook?"
- "i'm wondering if we just combine 02 and 03...they're so close we can probably make it one visual section"
- "would a blending mode not help with the white background?"
- "close! how wide is the entire left column? i'm wondering if we make it more narrow and leave addtional white space"

- "am i overthinking this or is it novel and interesting?"

Questions are native to the voice. Half his direction arrives as a

### How he's honest, including about himself

- "i was actually thinking smaller! let's try max 36"
- "seeing it now it feels TOO simple, let's make it another word (my bad)"
- "i'm honestly not sure which is the right thing to do here - i just want to make sure everything is all together, wrapped up and working"
- "not sure i have a strong opinion honestly"

- "i'm a little lost as to what we're even doing"
- "makes it sounds OLD...let's make sure we dont put dates in"

He says "honestly" and means it. He owns his own misses in parentheses

### How he describes the work

- "i build something almost everyday and it mostly comes from this external drive...to make the site feel a little 'alive'"
- "almost like a news feed of commits or builds or research"
- "i know it's different than the others but let's do what we can to align it to our new design system"
- "can we make it a slower draw and it ease at the end? and maybe they're one at a time?"
- "make them burn like the numbers - i realize you wont be able to read it but it's for the look at that point"
- "when a users clicks the logo in the middle - if there's a searching in the bar can it clear everything out and take them to the homepage?"

Plain words for sophisticated ideas. He never reaches for a bigger word
than the thing needs.

### Lines he wrote or chose for the site itself

- "Let's talk about what you have in mind and tailor a quote to your exact needs."
- "I love the work." (his replacement for "Available for work.")
- "Independent, Texas. Design and build." (approved as-is)

Note what these have in common: they are ordinary. When given a choice
between a clipped clever line and a plain warm one, he picks plain and
warm, every time.

## What this replaces

The failure mode is density. AI copy makes every sentence land a little
punch, every line audition to be a pull quote: "Same desk for all of
it." "The void belongs between and after, never before." No single line
breaks a rule. The texture is the tell.

So:

- One good line per section. The rest just talks.
- No buttons on the ends of lists. A list can simply end.
- "X, not Y" constructions: almost never. He doesn't talk in
  antithesis.
- Fragments are for his speech rhythm ("might be kinda cool"), not for
  gravitas ("One container. Zero video files.").
- Warmth comes from address and plainness, not from adjectives. "let's"
  and "you" do more than "delightful" ever will.

## Chat voice → site voice

The site is not lowercase chat. The translation that keeps the voice:

- Spelling and capitalization get fixed. Rhythm does not.
- Emoji and ":-)" stay in chat.
- Hedges get trimmed, not sterilized: "i kinda thought running it as a
  paragraph" → "Running it as a paragraph reads better." The looseness
  survives; the mumble goes.
- Enthusiasm survives at one beat per stretch, exactly like his chat.

Worked example, the homepage lede:

- AI: "Apps and ecommerce, campaigns and brand systems, photography and
  art direction, custom interiors, AI tools. Same desk for all of it."
- Him: the same list, then either nothing at all, or something he has
  actually said: "I build almost every day."

## Writing new copy

1. Get his raw words first — he talks or types rough, sloppy is fine.
   Composition from nothing is where the AI voice gets in.
2. Edit lightly: spelling, length, order. Do not restate.
3. Read it out loud. The one test decides.
