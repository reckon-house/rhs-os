# Stripe Press / Patrick Collison — STUB

**Status:** Stub. Add Stripe Press homepage copy and a book introduction or related Collison piece.

**Recommended captures:**
- Stripe Press homepage (https://press.stripe.com) — the introductory copy and the way the press positions itself
- Patrick Collison's personal site (https://patrickcollison.com) — the about page and the book recommendations page
- The introduction or foreword to one of the Stripe Press books (especially if it's a Collison-written intro)

**Use for:** literary and intellectually serious without being academic. Confident about ideas. The "writing IS the brand" register, where the prose itself signals taste rather than the visual treatment doing all the work. Especially useful as a reference for the long-form Reckon House pages where the writing has to carry the credibility.

**Specific moves to study:**
- Comfort with long sentences that use semicolons and asides to build a complex thought across a single span
- Specific references to books, ideas, or thinkers without explaining them — assumes the reader either knows or can look it up
- Restrained typography choices (the Stripe Press homepage and Collison's personal site both use a single typeface and let the writing carry the weight)
- The small typographic conventions: justified text, no decorative breaks, footnotes treated seriously
- Confident first-person on intellectual ground without performing erudition
- Lists used sparingly and only when the structure matters (book recommendations, project lists)
- The about page on Collison's site is a model of brevity that doesn't undersell

**When to reach for this:** before drafting any long-form page where the prose needs to signal seriousness. House Staples articles, the longer About page version, anywhere the writing is the primary brand surface. Especially useful as a counterweight when the Pentagram reference would push too far toward the agency-page register.

**Anti-pattern to watch for:** Stripe Press / Collison voice can read as Silicon Valley intellectualism — name-checking books and thinkers without enough texture to feel earned. The goal is the seriousness of the thinking, not the inventory of references.
