Reckon*House

Ways to show all of it at once

Density against focus, the whole range of the work, and nothing typed by hand. Every prototype runs on the same pieces, taken from your own files.

The idea under all six

The unit is a fragment, not a case study. A picture, a sentence, a figure, a spec line, a tool, a palette, a day from the daybook. The 30 studies, your About draft and the daybook come apart into 2,017 of them, made by a script from the files the site already has.

So the site updates itself. Add a study and its pieces join every view. What you curate is the rules each view lays pieces out by, plus a few moments you pick by hand.

And the work with no picture stops being a gap. A figure, a before and after, a stated time or a run of steps is a piece too, and each view gives it a form.

The prototypes

My read

The Cross-reference, two, is the one to judge now. The rooms are generated from each study's own pieces, so all 30 exist and stay in step with the real studies; a new study gets a room for free.

The Cross-reference is your brief taken literally: the whole practice as one screen of small lists, and one thing large on open paper. It is the one I would build the homepage from.

The Workings is the answer to the work with no picture. Every study on one schedule, so a six-week launch and a ten-year archive share an axis, and Sally OS finally has a shape.

The bigger point is that these are not six sites. They are six ways of looking at one library, and the site could offer them as views: the index to find, the catalogue to read, the map to see the whole career, the workings to see how, a brief to answer a need.
