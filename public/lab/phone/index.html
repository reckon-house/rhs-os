The index on a phone

On a desk the index and the room share the page: the index stays on the left, and while a study is open it is that study's spec sheet. A phone has room for one of them. Each concept below is one answer to where the other one goes, and how you move between the index, a shelf and a room, and from room to room. All of them run on the real index and the real rooms.

Seven ways, live

Each frame starts where its idea shows: a study open, the index over or beside it. Drag inside a frame as a finger would. Again starts a frame over; Open gives one the whole window, from the index, which is how to try it on a phone.

A · MenuOpen

Yours: "the TOC/index is a hamburger menu maybe?"

The index
The page opens on it. In a study, "Index" at the top brings it down over the room like a curtain, opened at the study you are in. Close lifts it, and the room is where you left it.
Next
Next at the foot of each room.
Good
Everyone knows it, and the room gets the whole glass.
Hard
The curtain covers the room, so you never see the index and the room together, and "Index" sits in the corner farthest from a thumb.

B · SheetOpen

Yours: "maybe it's at the bottom and slides up/open"

The index
The page opens on it. Open a study and it folds into a bar at the foot that says where you are, with a rule that fills as you read. Pull the bar up for half the index, opened at your study as its spec sheet; all the way for all of it.
Next
Next at the foot of each room, in the order of the shelf you came from.
Good
The index never leaves, and everything is under the thumb.
Hard
The bar costs 58px of glass, and the sheet must not fight the room's own scroll.

C · DrawerOpen

Yours: "or from the side?"

The index
It sits to the left of the room, as it does on the desk. A thin spine on the room's left edge, "Index" set sideways, pulls it in over most of the glass, the room kept as a sliver beside it.
Next
Next at the foot of each room.
Good
The desk's geometry, kept: index left, room right.
Hard
Safari uses a swipe from the left edge for its own back, so the tab has to do the work.

D · SwipeOpen

Yours: "maybe a swipe from side to side allows the user to go back and forth?"

The index
The page before the first room: a line's shelf, if you came from one. Tap the ribbon at the top to go straight back; the entry you came from is marked when you land.
Next
The rooms sit side by side in the shelf's order: swipe for the next or the one before. The ribbon shows how long the run is.
Good
Browsing is fast, like turning a magazine's pages.
Hard
The index only lives at the far left, so from the seventeenth study home is a tap on the ribbon, and there is no spec sheet while you read. A sideways swipe also competes with anything in a room that moves sideways.

E · ZoomOpen

Mine, from the desk's own move: a picture flies into the room's cover.

The index
A sheet of pictures, grouped by line, with the rest of the index under them. A picture flies up into its room's cover; the folio at the foot, or a pinch, flies it back into its place, marked.
Next
Next at the foot of each room.
Good
The pictures are the index, and the flight is the site's own move.
Hard
The index is never beside a room, so there is no spec sheet, and work without pictures reads weakest. Nobody finds a pinch unless told; the folio does the real work.

F · StackOpen

The phone's own model, and the desk's depths.

The index
Index, shelf, room, each pushed in from the right, with one head that names where Back goes. Back is a pull from the left edge, which lets you peek at the page underneath first; the phone's own back works too, because every depth has an address.
Next
Next at a room's foot swaps in the next study, so Back still returns to the shelf.
Good
Nothing to learn, and it matches the desk's depths one to one.
Hard
It is the most ordinary of the seven, and on an iPhone Safari's own back swipe shares that edge.

G · Sheet and swipeOpen

Mine: B's bar, with D's swipe on it.

The index
As in B: the bar at the foot, pulled up for the index.
Next
Swipe the bar sideways for the next study in the shelf, or the one before, as you would skip a song. The room itself never moves sideways, so nothing in it competes.
Good
One handle does both jobs, under the thumb.
Hard
The sideways swipe on the bar has to be found; a first visit might need a hint.

My read

G, which is B with D's swipe on its bar. It keeps the desk's idea that the index and the room are one page: the index is never more than a pull away, and the bar always says where you are. Moving between studies stays on the same handle, so the room's own scroll is never in question.

  • If the index should stay out of the way, A is the safe answer, and every visitor knows it.
  • If browsing matters most, D reads fastest, study to study.
  • If the pictures should lead, E makes the index a sheet of them.
