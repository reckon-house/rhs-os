Reckon*House

Seven scroll concepts, from two sites and the board

What normalisboring.es and akaru.fr do, what the board keeps, and seven ways to put them together. Every concept runs on the site's own words, pictures and colours.

What the two sites do

Normal is Boring

normalisboring.es · Awwwards Site of the Day

  • The ride. One pinned section about 17,000px wide, moved by about 9,000px of scrolling, so the page goes nearly two pixels sideways for every one down. Then about 4,000px of ordinary vertical page.
  • The turn. The row ends on a coral field exactly one screen wide. The pin lets go and that field slides up, and beige comes up under it with huge serif words building letter by letter.
  • Colour. Whole panels in white, black, beige #ECE4DA, powder blue #C7D7E9 and coral #DB5C59, with a teal dot for a cursor.
  • Type. A display serif around 100px in capitals, italic accents, and a 13px sans. Huge or tiny, nothing between, and a lot of white around both.
  • Chrome. The logo split across two corners, NORMAL top left and "is BORING" upside down bottom right. A Menú pill.
  • Details. Pictures unmask from one side as they arrive. A word runs vertically as a column's title. The footer's logo letters stretch taller as you reach the end.
  • Built with WordPress, GSAP (ScrollTrigger, ScrollSmoother, SplitText) and Lenis for the smooth scroll.

Akaru

akaru.fr · 35 Awwwards, 19 FWA

  • The opener. Half the screen is a giant condensed wordmark, one sentence and the socials under a rule. The other half is the projects as vertical strips, each in its own colour with one photo.
  • The turn. Nothing is released. One sticky block pans 2,770px sideways over the first 2,900px of scroll, then the same block moves 1,687px up over the next 5,000px. The camera changes direction mid-shot.
  • Strips. As a project reaches the left it opens to full width, its name set big at its foot. Blue-grey, mauve and sage, each project's own colour.
  • After. A column of project images runs up like a film strip beside a panel that holds still. Then a black awards section with counters in brackets, (35) and (19), and a 3D trophy.
  • Chrome. Nav items each with a coloured dot, pill buttons with a dot, a MENU pill. The footer ends on the wordmark rising out of black.
  • Built with Nuxt, Lenis and one WebGL canvas.

What they share: scrolling down moves the page sideways, smoothly. Whole panels of solid colour. Huge type against tiny labels with plenty of white. One moment where the direction changes, and a wordmark that performs at the end.

My read

A is the most complete answer to what you described: the ride, the colour fields, the white, and the turn into an ordinary page. It could end on D's turn instead of the black slide, the last word tipping onto its side as About's spine, which is the one move neither site has.

C is where the timeline could go. B and E are the bolder homepages. F holds the pieces, so any of them can be mixed into another.

H is your comp built out, and the one I would take forward. The word turning colour where it crosses a picture is what makes each turn read. Ogg shows here because it is installed on your Mac. It needs a web licence from Sharp Type before it can go live.

G is A and E together, the one to judge the whole idea by: a study read in full without leaving the page. It shows the cost too. Two studies make the route over 57,000px of scrolling, so the real version would run the full studies only for the leg you are on and keep the rest light.
