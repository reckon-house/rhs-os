# Voice line drafts — awaiting Jeremy

**Nothing in this file ships.** The build does not read it. These are
candidates in priority order from the 259-question audit; when a line
is right, move it into `voice-lines.ts` and run `npm run facts`.

Each entry says what the index ALREADY knows, so a line never wastes
itself repeating a count or a project name the machine will supply.
The house rule holds: **the say explains, the working counts.** No
numerals in a line unless it's a resume fact that will not drift
("8 years at Nordstrom" is fine; "7 marble projects" is not).

Where a line is written below, it is a **skeleton in your cadence, not
your opinion** — every one needs your redline or your replacement.

---

## Tier 1 — the questions that decide whether someone emails

These 13 audit questions currently fall through to a shrug. They are
the highest-value lines in the file and only you can write them.

### `rates` — "how much do you charge?", "what are your rates?", "what does a project cost?", "do you have a minimum?", "do you offer hourly?"

Index knows: nothing. This is entirely yours.
Needs: how you want to be asked. Even a deflection is an answer —
"Depends on scope, and I'd rather talk it through than post a number"
lands better than silence, and it routes to the contact card.

> _draft:_ "Depends entirely on scope. Tell me what you're thinking
> and I'll be straight with you about what it takes."

### `availability` — "are you available?", "are you taking new clients?", "what's your timeline?", "how long does a project take?"

Index knows: nothing. The practice note says "Available for work."
Needs: your actual posture right now, and whether you want this to
age (a specific "booking into fall" line needs maintenance; a general
one doesn't).

> _draft:_ "Usually yes, and usually a few weeks out. Ask me and I'll
> tell you honestly where the calendar sits."

### `process` — "what's your process?", "how do you work?", "do you have a team?", "is it just you?", "do you work with contractors?"

Index knows: nothing. This is the "one person" story A.R.C. tells.
Needs: yours. This is the single best place in the whole system to
make the independent-builder argument.

> _draft:_ "It's me. I design it and I build it, which is why the
> feedback loop is short. For interiors I work with trades I trust."
